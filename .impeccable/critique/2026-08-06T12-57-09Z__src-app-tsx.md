## Design Specificity Verdict

**Split. The reading layer is authored for this product. The homepage and archive chrome are category-interchangeable.**

**LLM assessment.** The article reader is genuinely specific and in places excellent. The brass drop cap, the `▪` end mark on the last paragraph, old-style figures on the dateline, the 68ch measure, the brass rule under the issue/page citation — these only make sense for a person's printed columns being re-set on screen. The tell that it was designed rather than decorated: `shortOpener` suppresses the drop cap when the first paragraph is under 60 characters (`App.tsx:2198`, `2311`), because a dateline would look absurd with a 3.4em brass initial. You only build that if you have looked at the material.

Then the homepage undoes it. Strip the name and it is a 2021 startup template: hero split with a portrait card and a rotated echo panel, a rounded search bar, a bento grid of 7 tinted cards each with an icon top-left and a count pill top-right and an arrow that widens its gap on hover, a rounded-2rem panel with a radial glow blob bleeding off the corner, three cards each opening with a 16:9 grey well holding one generic glyph, and a two-column feature-benefit block — "Gerçek Kaynaklar" / "Büyüyen Bir Arşiv" — describing the site's own sourcing policy under a heading promising academic research.

## Overall Impression

The reading experience is the best thing here and it is genuinely good. Everything around it is a well-built skeleton with no failure model. The single biggest opportunity is not visual: **the archive has a shape — 1968–1970 in socialist magazines, a long documentary gap, 2004–2012 at Zaman, 2017–2024 at P24 — and nothing in the interface knows it.** The hub grid says "617 yazı"; nothing says *when*, and nothing distinguishes "he did not write" from "we have not recovered it yet". That distinction is the whole positioning, and it is currently invisible.

## What's Working

**1. The reader's print vocabulary is earned, not decorative.** Drop cap + suppression logic, the `▪` end mark telling a reader of a scanned recovery that the text is complete rather than truncated, `onum` figures on the dateline. Every piece points the same direction.

**2. Filter state lives entirely in the URL.** `updateSearchParams` normalises defaults out of the querystring so a shared link is canonical; `Math.min(requestedPage, totalPages)` clamps a stale `?page=99` instead of showing an empty list. For an audience that includes researchers who need to send someone a filtered view, this is worth more than any on-page affordance — and the back button works correctly through a filter session, which almost nothing at this scale gets right.

**3. The accessibility foundation is better than the UX built on it.** Verified mechanically: 6/6 `<img>` with `alt`; **0 icon-only buttons without an accessible name** (all five carry `aria-label`); a real focus trap on the mobile drawer with `inert` on background, Tab/Shift-Tab wrap, Escape-to-close with focus return; `aria-current` on both nav trees; 3/3 `motion` sites guarded by `useReducedMotion` with a global CSS backstop; and 44px honoured on 17 interactive classes. Main text contrast passes AA comfortably in both themes (`--ink-soft` on `--bg`: 10.1 light / 10.6 dark).

## Priority Issues

### [P0] On mobile, applied filters are invisible — the search you just typed vanishes

**What:** `filtersOpen` initialises `false` (`App.tsx:1486`, `:1742`), `.archive-filter-panel { display: none }` below 900px (`index.css:1446`), and `ActiveFilterSummary` — the only surface naming the active filters — is rendered **inside** that panel (`App.tsx:1660`, `:1873`). The visible `.archive-toolbar` (`:1673`, `:1886`) carries only a count and a sort select.

**Why it matters:** the hero search navigates to `/tr/kose-yazilari?q=…`. A phone user lands on a page titled "Köşe Yazıları", count "37 yazı", 20 rows — with nothing on screen saying 632 pieces are hidden, and no way to clear it without discovering that search lives behind a button labelled "Filtreler". Every judgement they then make about the archive's size is wrong. This is the primary path from the homepage for the largest visitor segment.

**Fix:** move `ActiveFilterSummary` into `.archive-toolbar` at every breakpoint. Promote the search field out of the filter panel entirely — it is not a filter, it is the primary task — and give it its own always-visible row. Auto-open the panel when search params are non-empty on mount.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] No error surface: three silent redirects and one permanent dead loader

**What (all verified in source):**
- `NotFound` → `<Navigate to={home} replace />` (`App.tsx:2975`). There is no 404 page.
- An unresolved article slug → `<Navigate>` to the category index (`App.tsx:2191`).
- English item routes `/analyses/:slug`, `/interviews/:slug`, `/academic-articles/:slug` → `<Navigate to="/" replace />` (`App.tsx:2837`, `:2842`, `:2848`) — **even though the article exists at the Turkish path.**
- `useArticleBody`'s `.catch` only calls `console.error` (`App.tsx:2017`); `body` stays `undefined`, so the render falls to the `hasBody` branch and shows "Yazı yükleniyor…" **forever**.

**Why it matters:** `PRODUCT.md` names "the link to keep working" as a defining need of the researcher audience. A citation resolving to a 200 OK page showing the wrong content is worse for a scholarly record than a 404 — the reader gets no signal anything went wrong. The English→Turkish case is sharpest: the content is right there and the router throws the visitor to the homepage.

**Fix:** a real 404 in both languages stating the URL didn't resolve, with search and the section index. On an unresolved slug, render a message with search prefilled from the slug rather than redirecting. Point EN item routes at the Turkish equivalent instead of `/`. Add an `error` state to `useArticleBody` with a retry and the original-source link.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The Chronicle's headline contradicts its own chart

**What:** the lead hard-codes, in both languages, "2016'da bir kalem susturuldu; grafik bunu gösteriyor" / "In 2016 a pen was silenced — the chart shows it" (`App.tsx:2569`). **Verified against the data:** Turkish columns are Zaman **2004–2012** and P24 **2017–2024**. So on `/tr/kronik` the chart shows no 2016 stop, and shows him publishing continuously through 2024 — directly beneath a sentence saying he was silenced in 2016. The gap the chart *does* show (2013–2016) is an archive gap, not a biographical one: he wrote at Zaman until 2016, and the English Today's Zaman data covers those years.

Compounding it: `years` is the **union** of years-with-items and years-with-events (`App.tsx:2557`), so years with neither are omitted entirely. The spine jumps 1971 → 1980 → 1997 → 2002. Every bar is compared against a neighbour that may be nine years away, and the silent years — the thing the page exists to show — have been deleted from the axis.

**Why it matters:** this is the only data visualisation on the site, the page most likely to be screenshotted, and it makes an emotive historical claim its own data refutes. It also violates the site's own stated stance twice over — "no victim language" (passive-voice martyrdom) and never drawing an inference from incomplete data.

**Fix:** replace the lead's conclusion with the range and total only; narrate the imprisonment as an event row where the data supports it, per language. Render a continuous year axis from first to last year — zero-count years get an empty track, not omission — and mark stretches where zero means "not yet recovered" rather than "did not write". That distinction is the most important thing this page can teach, and it currently teaches the opposite.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] "Benden Seçkiler" attributes a PRNG shuffle to Şahin Alpay

**What:** `weeklyPicks` seeds `mulberry32` with the ISO week and shuffles the pool (`App.tsx:714-728`); the section is titled "Benden Seçkiler" — literally *selections from me*.

**Why it matters:** the articles are real, but **the label is the fabrication**. A reader reasonably concludes he chose these three, this week. He is alive and can be asked; this is the one claim on the site its own subject could contradict. It quietly undermines the positioning — a first-party archive maintained by the author's own side — by putting words in his mouth in first person.

**Fix:** either make it true (a small owner-approved `pickIds` array, no PRNG) or rename it to what it is: "Arşivden" / "From the archive", with a kicker saying three pieces rotating weekly. The second is a one-line change.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Two real accessibility defects the visual review missed

**What (both from the deterministic pass):**
1. **The search inputs may have no visible focus ring.** `outline: none` is set on `.hero-search-field input` (`index.css:729`) and `.search-field input` (`index.css:1509`), both *after* the global `:focus-visible` rule (`index.css:247`), and `grep 'focus-within' src/index.css` returns **zero hits** — so there is no compensating wrapper style. Keyboard users tabbing into the site's primary control may get no indication of where they are. (Not browser-verified — no browser tool this session.)
2. **Control borders fail WCAG 1.4.11.** `--line-strong` is bound to `--control-border` and is the visible boundary of `.lang-toggle`, `.mobile-menu-toggle` and `.theme-toggle`. Contrast against the page: **2.1:1 light, 2.5:1 dark** — both below the 3:1 required for UI component boundaries.

Also: `.footer-links a` is the one interactive class with no 44px floor that is reachable on touch (`index.css:2318`); heading levels skip h1→h3 on the archive and books pages and h2→h4 in `AcademicHeritage`; and `--muted` on `--surface-mid` in dark theme is 4.4:1, marginally under AA.

**Why it matters:** the accessibility foundation here is genuinely strong, which makes these four gaps the difference between "good" and "correct" for the older, keyboard- and zoom-dependent readers the product explicitly designs for.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P2] The homepage's dominant visual content is eight empty placeholder wells

**What:** `PORTRAIT = null` → a 380px monogram box; three 16:9 grey wells each holding one generic glyph; `HERITAGE_PHOTOS = [null × 4]` → four graph-paper tiles closing the page. The detector independently flagged the portrait placeholder's grid-line gradient as the generic-AI-background pattern (`index.css:777`).

**Why it matters:** keeping the frames converts a correct principle (no stock photography) into a visible confession of incompleteness at the two highest-attention positions on the site. It also contradicts the system's own rule that empty sections read as *pending record* — these read as *unfinished build*.

**Fix:** delete the heritage photo column and let the copy run full-width, or replace the block with something only this product could show — a compact 1968–2024 coverage strip with outlet bands and gaps marked, doubling as navigation into the Chronicle. ## Questions to Consider

1. **The archive has a shape. Why does nothing in the interface know it?** A single horizontal coverage band — 1968–2024, outlet bands, gaps marked — would tell a first-time visitor more in one second than the entire hub grid does, and it is a composition no other product could use.
2. **If the empty half is normal for years, why is it designed as an absence rather than a commitment?** "Çok yakında" is a marketing phrase on a historical record. What would "Cumhuriyet — 1980'ler, henüz çıkarılmadı. Kaynaklar TÜSTAV üzerinden izleniyor." look like? It turns a hole into evidence of method — which is the entire positioning.
3. **What if search came before filtering, everywhere?** Search is currently the fourth thing inside a collapsed panel called "Filters". For 1,080 items with a readership that arrives knowing what it wants, search *is* the page. The 15-control sidebar serves an imagined power user; the real one is a 71-year-old trying to remember a headline.
4. **Would a confident version show the portrait frame at all, given there is no portrait?** Every empty well is a small apology. What would the front page look like if it showed only what exists — the name, the sentence, the search, and the shape of the archive?
5. **The Chronicle says "a pen was silenced" while the chart shows him publishing through 2024. Which should a visitor believe?** And: is the honest version — that the 2013–2016 gap is *your* gap, not his — actually more moving than the dramatized one?
6. **Who is "Benden Seçkiler" speaking as?** If it is him, it should be him. If it is the archive, it should say so. There is no third option on a site whose first principle is that nothing is presented as something it isn't.
7. **What should a reader do at the end of an article?** On a body of work written as running commentary on a country, "the next column he wrote" is the native reading mode, and the data to support it is already sorted.
