## What's Working

**1. Failure states are designed, not defaulted.** `useArticleBody` models loading/loaded/failed as three real states with a retry that genuinely refires, and B confirmed the failure branch is checked *before* the loading branch, so a rejected fetch can no longer hang forever. `DeadEnd` pre-seeds a search from the failed slug. `CiteThis` degrades to "select and copy the text below" when the clipboard is blocked.

**2. Filter state is the URL, and the URL is shown back to the user.** Defaults are normalised out of the query string; arriving on a filtered URL auto-opens the panel; the active-filter summary sits above the list at every breakpoint with per-filter dismissal. A refresh, a bookmark or a pasted link all reconstruct the exact view.

**3. The coverage strip is the most honest object on the site.** Every band derives from parsed dates; no span is asserted for unrecovered material; it deliberately spans both languages because filtering by UI language would show an English reader one paper instead of the real body of work.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Turkish case-folding makes correct searches return nothing

Verified directly: `"İstanbul".toLowerCase()` is `"i̇stanbul"` (i + U+0307), which does not contain `"istanbul"`. Searching `istanbul`, `isik` or `universite` against text containing İstanbul / IŞIK / Üniversitesi returns **zero results, with no error**.

`matchesFilters` compares `archiveItemText(...).toLowerCase().includes(search.toLowerCase())` (`App.tsx:1229`). The primary audience searches in Turkish, and İ/I/ı/i are among the most common letters in the corpus. The archive silently answers "he never wrote about that". For a permanent record, a confident false negative is the worst possible failure.

**Fix:** a folding helper beside `parseTurkishDate`, applied to both sides — map `İ`→`i`, `I`→`ı`, then `toLocaleLowerCase('tr')`, `normalize('NFD')`, strip `̇`; fold `ı/i ş/s ğ/g ü/u ö/o ç/c` to bare forms so a reader without a Turkish keyboard can still find `Gülen`.

**Command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P0] Every citation the site generates points at a domain that does not exist

`pageUrl()` hardcodes `https://sahinalpay.net` (`App.tsx:228`), feeding `buildCitation`, the copy-on-select attribution appended to any selection over 40 characters, every canonical/og:url, and the JSON-LD `@id` on Person, Article and Breadcrumb. PRODUCT.md records that the site is published on `sahinalpay.com` and that `.net` was never registered.

The Cite button hands a journalist a permanently dead URL formatted to look authoritative, with an access date on it. A wrong citation in print is unrecoverable.

**Fix:** the domain flip itself is the owner's decision and must change `index.html`, `scripts/generate-sitemap.mjs` and `public/llms.txt` together — so the concrete action is to put that decision in front of the owner. Until it lands, build the citation URL from `window.location.origin` so a copied citation at least matches the site the reader is on.

**Command:** owner decision, then `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The published privacy notice describes storage the code never performs

`useTheme` holds theme in React state and writes `data-theme` to the root — it never touches `localStorage`. Verified: the only persisted keys are `lang` and `cookie-consent`. Both language versions of the Cookie & Data Notice state that the site "saves two functional items in your browser's local storage: your language preference and your light/dark theme preference".

Two failures at once. An older reader who switches theme loses it on every reload and every new tab, on a site that names unhurried interaction as a functional requirement. And a KVKK/GDPR disclosure describing processing that does not occur is a defective notice, linked from a persistent banner.

**Fix:** read the stored theme in the `useState` initialiser and write it in `toggle`, keyed `'theme'` with `'light' | 'dark' | null` (null = follow system), wrapped in try/catch like `readConsent`. Persisting is the behaviour the product already promised.

**Command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Changing page leaves the reader at the bottom of the previous one

`MainShell`'s scroll reset fires on `location.pathname` only (`App.tsx:2952`); pagination changes a search parameter, so nothing resets. Clicking "Sonraki" on page 3 of 34 leaves the viewport where it was, now showing the tail of page 4 with the count and pagination off-screen above. The same gap applies to every filter and sort change.

This is the archive's main browsing loop and the audience skews older. It reads as "the button did nothing".

**Fix:** a second effect keyed on `location.search` that scrolls the top of `.archive-main` into view with `behavior: 'instant'` (never a bare `scrollTo` — `html` sets `scroll-behavior: smooth`), and move focus to the `sr-only` "Results" heading so the change is announced.

**Command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] Copy still makes claims the data does not support

One root cause, five instances: **"Benden Seçkiler" / "My Picks"** names a week-seeded `mulberry32` shuffle as a first-person selection; the **English hub** promises Turkish source material and links to `/tr/soylesiler` and `/tr/akademik-makaleler`, both of which hold zero items; the **facts strip** says 5 outlets in English and 8 in Turkish and shows **"∞ Columns written"** beside exact counts; **"Coming soon"** is promotional register on a record that may take a decade; and the **chronicle lead** still reads "2016'da bir kalem susturuldu; grafik bunu gösteriyor" — dramatization against the stated no-victim-language rule, asserting a causal reading of a dip that is partly unrecovered material.

Each is small. Together they teach a careful reader that the copy layer is not held to the standard the archive layer is — which devalues the archive layer, and that traceability is the product's only real differentiator.

**Sam — screen reader + keyboard only**
Well served: skip link, `inert` on background, Esc + focus return, the new `sr-only` "Filters"/"Results" headings, `aria-current`, live regions on count and search status. Breaks: external rows carry `aria-label={title + "(opens in a new tab)"}`, which **overrides** the row's inner text, so date, outlet and excerpt are never announced — internal rows announce all of it. The article toolbar is three consecutive icon-only buttons whose only differentiation is `aria-label`, and the current font size lives only in an `sr-only` `<output>`, so a low-vision sighted user gets no feedback. Paginating changes the list with no focus move and no announcement. `.tag` items look like interactive chips but are inert `<li>`s.

**Casey — one-handed, phone, 4G, interrupted**
Well served: filters collapse below 900px, search stays visible outside the panel, the 44px floor holds on essentially every control, and filter state survives a tab switch because it lives in the URL. Breaks: the first keystroke of any search triggers `loadOutletBodies` across *every* outlet on the page — the Zaman and P24 body chunks — on a cellular connection. Primary actions are all top-of-screen; nothing is in the thumb zone. The cookie notice is `position: fixed; bottom: 0` over the pagination controls and offers Accept only, no close. Returning after an interruption loses scroll position and font size.

## Minor Observations

- **The coverage strip is inert.** It is the clearest IA object on the site and the most obvious place a visitor would click to filter. Linking each band to `?outlet=…&from=…&to=…` would turn the home page's best explanation into its best entry point.
- **Blue that does nothing:** `.coverage-outlet` is `--accent-ink` static text, against the One Accent Rule.
- **A disabled CTA sits above the books** — "View book summaries ↗" plus "External link to be added", before any actual book.
- **Empty sections render a full filter apparatus over nothing** — interviews and academic articles get search, year range, source chips, sort and "0 yazı".
- **Outlet chips carry no counts**, so emptiness must be discovered by clicking Cumhuriyet, Sabah and Milliyet one at a time.
- **Language switch discards article context** — an English reader mid-column lands on a one-outlet index with no note.
- **`archive-row-badge` is 0.68rem** (~12.2px), below the system's own smallest documented role, on a site whose stated load case is older readers.
- **`?page=99` on a 34-page list** silently renders page 34 while the URL keeps saying 99.
- **The footer never shows the email address** — the link text is just "E-posta"/"Email".
- **`relatedArticles` re-parses ~1,080 dates on every article render**, on the site's peak surface.
- **`AcademicHeritage` is named for a section it no longer renders** (it renders "Archive Coverage") — naming drift that will mislead the next edit.

## Questions to Consider

1. **If the archive's job is "find a specific piece", why is there no search that crosses sections and languages?** Search is scoped per page, and the English hero can only ever reach 411 of 1,080 items. One archive-wide search with facets would collapse the hub grid, the duplicated filter code, and three of the four cognitive-load failures into a single surface.
2. **What if the coverage strip were the home page?** It is the only element that answers "what is this and what does it hold" at a glance, it is derived entirely from real data, and it currently sits as a decorative figure in the fifth section down next to a marketing feature pair.
3. **The empty half will be empty for years — what does "pending record" actually look like?** Right now it looks like "Coming soon", a disabled button, and a filter panel over zero rows. If half the design is permanently the empty state, what would it look like to state what is *known to exist and not yet recovered* — the years, the paper, the reason — instead of an unkept promise?
4. **Who chooses the weekly picks?** Either a person does, and the section should say who and stop shuffling, or a machine does, and it should not be called "Benden".
5. **What does this site owe a reader who prints an article?** For a permanent record consulted by researchers, paper is a real output. Today the answer is a progress bar, a sticky nav, and a cookie banner.
